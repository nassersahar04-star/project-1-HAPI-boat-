Graduation Project

Autonomous Green-Powered Water Cleaning Boat

A self-driving, battery-powered boat that collects floating waste from our waterways and helps keep Egypt’s water clean.

Who we are

We are a team of seven students working on our graduation project. Our team covers planning, research, simulation, electronics and implementation, and we share one goal: protecting our waterways with clean, practical technology.

The problem

Floating waste in canals, rivers and seas harms water quality, wildlife and the economy.

Polluted waterways

Plastic and other floating trash gather on the water surface and keep spreading.

Slow manual cleaning

Cleaning by hand or with crewed boats takes a lot of time, effort and people.

Costly and polluting

Fuel-powered cleaning vessels are expensive to run and add emissions of their own.

Hard to reach

Some areas are difficult or unsafe for workers to reach regularly.

How our project solves it

We designed an autonomous boat that cleans the water surface by itself, using clean energy and low-cost electronics.

1

Autonomous

The boat moves and works on its own, with no crew needed on board.

2

Smart control

An ESP32 microcontroller controls the boat’s movement and electronics.

3

Green-powered

The prototype runs on batteries, with solar power planned as a future upgrade.

4

Designed and simulated

Circuits were designed and simulated in Proteus, with a block diagram guiding the build.

Our goal

To make cleaning our waterways cheaper, faster and greener.

  • Reduce floating waste in water bodies without putting workers at risk.
  • Replace fuel-based cleaning with clean energy.
  • Build a working prototype and grow it with solar power.
  • Work with partners such as the Suez Canal Authority, who showed interest in the idea.
